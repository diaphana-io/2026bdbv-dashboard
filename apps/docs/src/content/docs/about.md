## Who we are

### Matt Sakamaki, MD, MPH

Matt Sakamaki is a physician from Japan and holds a Master of Public Health from the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, with focus areas in epidemiology, social epidemiology, and behavioral sciences.

He trained at the School of Medicine, Niigata University (2016–2023), then worked as a junior resident and staff physician in nephrology at Kashiwazaki General Medical Center (JA Niigata Koseiren, 2023–2025), providing community-based care in a rural, super-aging setting. There he saw that long-standing health promotion activities often lacked quantitative evaluation, and that many drivers of local health disparities sit outside routine clinical and administrative data. That experience led him to public health: closing the gap between social-determinants research and practical tools clinicians and communities can use.

His interests include evidence-based community interventions, how social norms and health attitudes form, and how vaccination and other preventive behaviors are understood across community, family, and individual levels in different cultural contexts. He maintains this outbreak documentation site and the companion [interactive dashboard](https://2026bdbv.diaphana.io/app) as an open project at [diaphana-io/2026bdbv-dashboard](https://github.com/diaphana-io/2026bdbv-dashboard).

## What is this dashboard?

The [2026 BDBV outbreak dashboard](https://2026bdbv.diaphana.io/app) is an English-language interactive application that summarizes how Bundibugyo virus disease spread from the Democratic Republic of the Congo to Uganda, Kenya, and Nigeria during the 2026 outbreak.

It provides outbreak overview, timeline, cases, contacts, map, border measures, and sources. Production data come from curated real-world sources and human-approved claims. When two sources disagree, both claims stay visible until a human curator resolves the conflict. Automated extract stays off in production until a backend design is approved through the project’s issue tracker.

This `/docs` site is the public companion: short background and references for a general audience. It does not replace WHO or national ministry reporting. For live maps and curated claims, use the [dashboard](https://2026bdbv.diaphana.io/app).
